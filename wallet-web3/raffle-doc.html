01 Overview

A raffle holds other people's APE and someone else's NFT while it runs. That's a different bar than the Cultomizer, which only ever touches a wallet's own token — so this ships as its own repo, on its own release schedule, not bolted onto wallet-web3. A different domain was the original ask; the real reason to separate it is that a bug here costs someone else's money, not just a broken page.

02 How a raffle runs

1 · SetupOrganizer deposits the NFT into the contract
2 · SellingTickets go on sale, 5 max per wallet
3 · TriggerCap is hit, or the organizer forces the start
4 · DrawvRNG requested → fulfilled ~later
5 · SettleNFT → winner. APE → dev / treasury, claimable
Escape hatch If the deadline passes before the cap is hit and nobody forced the start, the raffle closes itself: the NFT goes back to whoever deposited it, and every ticket buyer can claim their APE back. No one has to be online for this — it isn't a person deciding to cancel, it's a rule firing on its own.

03 Rules, locked in

RuleSettingWhy
Max tickets / wallet5Stops the obvious case. Doesn't stop someone splitting across wallets — nothing on-chain can.
Organizer buys ticketsNot allowedOtherwise forcing an early draw lets the organizer win their own NFT back and keep the other buyers' APE.
Force-start before capOrganizer only, any timeDoesn't help them — fewer tickets sold means higher odds for whoever already bought in, and less revenue for the organizer. The incentive already points at waiting.
Force-start with 0 ticketsDraw skipped, NFT returnedCan't pick a winner from an empty list.
Nobody ever forces itDeadline is the backstopPast it, anyone can close the raffle. Nothing stays stuck waiting on one wallet.
Cancel at willNot possibleThe deadline is the only exit. No one can pull a raffle after tickets are already sold.

04 Randomness

Chainlink VRF isn't deployed on ApeChain — its supported list stops at Arbitrum, Base, Ronin, Soneium and a handful of others, no Orbit L3s. ApeChain runs its own native vRNG instead: no subscription to fund ahead of time, no whitelist. The contract just quotes a fee in APE via getFee() and pays it per request.

It's two transactions, not one: requestRandomness() fires the moment the cap is hit or the organizer forces the start — ticket sales lock right there — and fulfillRandomness() lands the number a while after. That gap is what gives the "results about an hour later" the community was told.

Two things the contract has to get right here: guard fulfillRandomness() so only the vRNG contract's address can call it — open it up and anyone can call it with whatever number makes them win. And reserve the vRNG fee out of ticket revenue before the dev/treasury split, or a raffle can end up without enough APE left to pay for its own draw.

05 Open — the split

Dev / treasury split on ticket revenue

needs a number

Proposed out loud: 5 APE/ticket, "60/30" as a split, with 75 APE to dev and 175 APE to treasury on a 50-ticket raffle.

75 (dev) + 175 (treasury) = 250 APE = 50 tickets × 5 APE ✓
75 / 250 = 30% · 175 / 250 = 70%
→ that's 30/70, not the 60/30 that was said. Same numbers, different split than stated — confirm which one's actually meant before it's hardcoded.

Also worth deciding: fixed forever, or adjustable later behind an admin call with a hard ceiling (e.g. "dev cut can never exceed 30%") so the community can see the limit even if the exact number moves?

06 Open — one raffle or many

Fixed parameters vs. a configurable raffle factory

changes the build size

Making ticket price and ticket count configurable per raffle — as asked — isn't a settings tweak. It changes what gets built.

Fixed
  • Price & cap baked in at deploy
  • One contract, one raffle at a time
  • Smallest surface to test and audit
  • Ships fast
Configurable
  • A factory: new raffles spun up with their own price / cap / prize
  • Needs access control — who's allowed to create one
  • Needs the admin panel itself, a second webapp
  • Real flexibility, real extra weeks

Either is buildable. This just has to be picked before the contract's shape is decided — it isn't a parameter you add later.

07 Repo & build order

  • New repo, separate from wallet-web3 / backend — third-party funds and NFTs don't share a deploy with the Cultomizer.
  • Contract + tests first. The tests are the proof that every rule in §3 actually holds — max tickets per wallet, organizer locked out, zero-ticket force-start, the deadline backstop.
  • Full raffle end-to-end on Curtis (ApeChain testnet) — deposit, buy, force or fill, draw, claim — before anything touches mainnet.
  • The webapp to run it comes after the contract is solid, not alongside it.

08 Before real APE goes in

  • External audit. Non-negotiable once it's holding someone else's funds and NFT — an error here doesn't get a hotfix.
  • The NFT has to be sitting in the contract before ticket sales open — never left in the organizer's wallet where it could be sold or moved mid-raffle.
  • The treasury address should be a multisig, not one wallet — that's the difference between the community seeing a treasury and seeing one person's balance with a different label.
